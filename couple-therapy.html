Is couple counselling right for you?

Most couples reach out when one or more of these feels familiar.

  • The same argument keeps repeating with no resolution
  • Long silences, or one of you shuts down completely
  • Trust has been shaken and you are unsure how to rebuild it
  • In-law or joint family expectations are creating friction
  • Emotional or physical intimacy has faded
  • Things changed after a baby, a move, or a job shift
  • Money conversations turn into blame
  • You care about each other but feel like roommates

You do not need a crisis to start. Many couples come simply because they want to stop drifting apart.

Common questions before you start

The things most couples want to know before that first message.

Do both partners need to attend?
Ideally yes, because couple therapy works best when both perspectives are in the room. That said, many people begin alone when their partner is hesitant, and that is a completely valid starting point. Individual sessions can still create meaningful change in the relationship.
How many sessions will we need?
It depends on what you are working through. Most couples notice a shift in tone within the first three to four sessions. Ashwini will share a realistic sense of the roadmap after the first conversation, and you decide the pace from there.
What does a session cost?
Current session fees are shared over WhatsApp before you book, so there are no surprises. Message us and you will get a clear answer straight away.
Are online sessions as effective as in-person?
For most couples, yes. Online sessions work especially well when partners travel, work different shifts, or live in different cities. Sessions are available both online and in person in Chennai.
Is everything we say confidential?
Yes. Everything discussed stays strictly between you and your therapist, in line with professional ethical guidelines. The only exceptions are situations involving immediate risk to safety, which would be explained to you clearly.
Which languages are sessions offered in?
English, Tamil, Kannada, and Tulu. Many couples find it easier to express difficult feelings in their mother tongue, and you are welcome to switch between languages during a session.
What actually happens in the first session?
Mostly listening. Ashwini will ask about what brought you in, how things have been between you, and what each of you hopes for. There is no pressure to share more than you are ready to, and no homework on day one.
Will the therapist take sides?
No. The role is to hold a neutral, balanced space where both partners feel heard. The aim is not to decide who is right, but to understand the pattern that keeps both of you stuck.
