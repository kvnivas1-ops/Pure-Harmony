When family conflict enters your marriage

Marriage Counselling for In-Law Problems

You love your family, and you want your marriage to work. But when disagreements with parents or in-laws keep coming between you and your spouse, it can feel as though you are being forced to choose sides.

Perhaps visits, household decisions, childcare, finances or comments from relatives keep turning into arguments. One partner may feel unsupported, while the other feels caught between a spouse and their parents. Over time, the original disagreement can become a deeper question: are we a team?

Does any of this feel familiar?

If these experiences sound close to what you are going through, you do not have to figure everything out on your own.

  • You feel your spouse does not stand up for you when a family member crosses a boundary.
  • Your partner feels you are asking them to distance themselves from their parents.
  • Decisions about visits, money, childcare or living arrangements cause repeated conflict.
  • Private disagreements are shared with relatives, making matters worse.
  • You avoid discussing family because it almost always ends in a fight.

How counselling may help

Marriage counselling offers a neutral space to understand the impact of family conflict on the couple. Sessions can help each partner explain what loyalty, respect and support mean to them, identify specific situations that need attention and discuss boundaries that are clear and realistic. The goal is not to label a parent or relative as the problem, but to help the couple respond as a team.

Understanding your relationship and family context

In many Indian and South Indian families, relationships with parents and extended family are important and ongoing. Financial support, caregiving, festivals, shared homes and expectations around respect can carry real emotional weight. Counselling should recognise these realities while making room for the couple's privacy and independent decisions.

Is this the right time to seek support?

You do not need to wait for a major family rupture. If the same issue keeps returning or either partner feels alone in managing family pressure, a guided conversation may help. Both partners are encouraged to participate where appropriate.

Frequently asked questions

Will counselling tell my spouse to choose me over their parents?

No. The aim is not to force a choice or cut off family relationships. It is to help partners understand each other and agree on healthy, workable boundaries.

Can I come if my spouse refuses to attend?

Contact Pure Harmony to discuss whether an individual consultation is an appropriate first step. A joint process may be considered if both partners are willing.

Can counselling help when we live in a joint family?

It can help couples discuss privacy, responsibilities, decision-making and expectations while recognising the practical realities of shared living.
